Question 1Skipped

A specialty chemicals manufacturer's transformation office is trying to expand AI initiatives for forecasting, quality inspection, and customer service. Production, safety, commercial, and finance leaders define success differently and worry that another function will control priorities. Several pilots have stalled because business owners will not commit staff time or share decision authority.

Which leadership action should the transformation office recommend?
Pause AI funding until every function approves the plan. Resume pilots only after all objections are removed in reviews.
Explanation
This action treats cultural resistance as a prerequisite that must be fully eliminated before learning can continue. Waiting for unanimous comfort can deepen risk aversion and delay the organizational learning that AI adoption requires.
Correct answer
Form a cross-functional AI steering group. Align teams on shared outcomes, decision roles, and change expectations.
Explanation
This action addresses the cultural barrier of siloed ownership and misaligned priorities. AWS guidance emphasizes that repeatable AI value depends on collaboration across stakeholders, business units, and practices, not only on technology selection.
Move AI decisions to the technology office alone. Ask business teams to adopt approved solutions through standard updates.
Explanation
This action centralizes control in a technology function and reduces business ownership. AWS CAF-AI advises organizations to move beyond a purely technological view of AI adoption and to involve the people who drive and guardrail initiatives.
Let each department select an AI tool independently. Compare adoption rates after separate pilots and budget cycles finish.
Explanation
This action might reduce short-term conflict by allowing each function to move separately. However, independent tool selection reinforces siloed behavior and does not create shared accountability for enterprise AI adoption.
Overall explanation

A common cultural barrier to AI adoption is not lack of interest in AI, but lack of organizational alignment. When functions define value differently or fear that another group will control priorities, pilots can stall even when the technology is promising.

The AWS Cloud Adoption Framework for Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Generative AI frames AI adoption as an enterprise transformation effort, not a technology rollout alone. Its People perspective emphasizes culture, collaboration, shared language, talent, and leadership as foundations for reliable and repeatable AI value. See AWS Cloud Adoption Framework for Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Generative AI and People perspective: Culture and change towards AI/ML-first.

A cross-functional AI steering group helps leaders align on business outcomes, decision roles, and change expectations before scaling initiatives. In contrast, independent departmental tool choices, unanimous preapproval, or technology-only control can preserve the very cultural barriers that slow adoption: silos, risk aversion, and weak business ownership.

Question 2Skipped

An online education provider's student support team plans to add a generative AI tutor to a teen learning portal. Leaders are concerned that the tutor could produce hateful, sexual, violent, or self-harm content during student conversations. The provider wants a consistent preventive control that can be applied before content is shown to students, without requiring staff to review every response manually.

Which action should the provider take?
Retrain the model on the provider's curriculum materials before student access.
Explanation
Retraining or fine-tuning may adapt a model to a domain, but it is not a reliable content safety control by itself. A curriculum-focused model can still produce unsafe responses without safeguards that evaluate prompts and outputs.
Correct answer
Apply Amazon Bedrock Guardrails with content filters and denied topics for the tutor.
Explanation
This action applies an automated safety layer that can help block or filter harmful prompts and responses before students see them. Amazon Bedrock Guardrails supports content filters for common harmful categories and denied topics for use-case-specific restrictions.
Publish the tutor with user reporting and remove unsafe conversations after review.
Explanation
User reporting is reactive because harmful content can reach students before anyone reviews it. This approach does not meet the need for a preventive control before content is shown.
Add a disclaimer that responses are machine generated and may contain mistakes.
Explanation
A disclaimer can set user expectations, but it does not filter or block harmful content. This approach shifts risk awareness to users instead of reducing unsafe outputs before exposure.
Overall explanation

For a student-facing generative AI experience, the business need is not only accuracy, it is preventing harmful content from reaching users. Amazon Bedrock Guardrails is designed to add safeguards to generative AI applications by applying controls such as content filters and denied topics to prompts and model responses. These controls help reduce categories of unsafe content and restrict subjects that an organization does not want the application to address.

Human review, user reporting, and disclaimers can be useful parts of a broader responsible AI program, but they do not provide the same preventive protection. User reporting happens after exposure, disclaimers do not change model behavior, and retraining for domain fit does not guarantee that unsafe content will be blocked. The practical governance pattern is to combine business policy with technical safeguards that consistently enforce content boundaries at runtime. For more information, see Amazon Bedrock Guardrails.

Question 3Skipped

An environmental consulting firm's proposal review board is comparing AI sourcing proposals for an internal assistant that helps consultants draft client reports from approved field observations and template language. Leadership wants a first release this quarter, has a limited budget, and does not see model building as a competitive differentiator. The firm needs room to customize the assistant for its reporting style and to keep human review before client delivery.

Which TWO recommendations should the board make?
Select the lowest-cost packaged chatbot before defining report-quality and cycle-time measures for the pilot.
Explanation
A low-cost packaged option might be attractive, but selecting it before defining business measures skips the value assessment. AWS strategy guidance emphasizes working backward from business problems and measurable impact before choosing a sourcing path.
Correct selection
Advance a partner proposal that uses Amazon Bedrock to build the assistant with FM choice and customization.
Explanation
This recommendation fits a scenario that needs generative AI application development and room to customize without building a model from scratch. A partner can help close capability and timeline gaps while the organization focuses on the business outcome and review process.
Fund an internal foundation model program to preserve full control over a non-differentiating reporting use case.
Explanation
This recommendation reflects a technology-first and not invented here mindset. Full internal model development can be valuable for differentiated capabilities, but it conflicts with the stated budget, timeline, and lack of strategic need for model ownership.
Postpone sourcing until the team can build the assistant without partner or vendor involvement for the first release.
Explanation
This recommendation delays value delivery and ignores the stated limited internal capability and short timeline. Partnering or adopting existing capabilities can be appropriate when internal build capacity is not aligned with the desired business outcome.
Correct selection
Deprioritize SageMaker AI model training unless proprietary model ownership is strategically differentiating.
Explanation
Amazon SageMaker AI is appropriate when the goal is to build, train, and deploy ML models, including foundation models, at scale. In this scenario, model building is not a differentiator, so training a proprietary model would likely add cost and schedule risk without matching the business need.
Overall explanation

For AI sourcing decisions, the useful business frame is not simply build everything or buy everything. AWS AI strategy guidance recommends working backward from the customer or business problem, defining measurable impact, and then deciding whether to build, tune, or adopt an existing AI system. As an AI portfolio grows, the buy-versus-build decision becomes especially important because a not invented here mindset can consume budget and slow delivery without creating differentiated value. See Business Perspective: The AI strategy in the age of AI/ML.

In this scenario, the organization needs a near-term generative AI assistant with customization, but it does not need to own or train a proprietary model. The AWS generative AI decision guide positions Amazon Bedrock for foundation model choice, customization, and building generative AI applications, while Amazon SageMaker AI is for building, training, and deploying ML models, including foundation models, at scale. That makes a partner-led Bedrock approach more aligned to the business constraints, while SageMaker AI model training should be reserved for a case where custom model development is strategically justified. See AWS Generative AI Decision Guide.

Question 5Skipped

A regional utilities provider's customer engagement team plans to use AI to personalize energy-efficiency offers based on smart meter readings, billing history, and household demographics. The provider serves customers across jurisdictions with different privacy and consumer protection rules. The team wants to reduce regulatory compliance risk before launch.

Which action should the team take?
Deploy the model to a limited customer group, track complaint volumes, and expand after engagement improves.
Explanation
A limited launch can reduce business exposure, but it does not establish that the data use is lawful or properly disclosed. Monitoring complaints is reactive and might identify harm only after a compliance issue has already occurred.
Ask the AI vendor to certify model accuracy, retain all available customer records, and document campaign outcomes.
Explanation
Model accuracy does not resolve whether the organization can use customer data for personalization under applicable rules. Retaining all available records conflicts with the principle of using only the data needed for the approved business purpose.
Remove customer names from model outputs, keep all source attributes, and rely on internal access approvals.
Explanation
Removing names from outputs does not necessarily make smart meter readings, billing history, or demographic data nonpersonal. Internal access approvals are security controls, but they do not address purpose limitation, notice, consent, or data minimization requirements.
Correct answer
Conduct a privacy impact assessment, verify permitted data uses, and minimize customer attributes used for offers.
Explanation
This action addresses the compliance risk before customer data is processed for a new AI-driven purpose. A privacy impact assessment helps identify whether the planned use is allowed, whether customers need notice or consent, and whether unnecessary personal data can be excluded.
Overall explanation

AI compliance risk is not limited to whether a model performs well. When a business process uses personal or sensitive customer data, leaders must consider privacy, consent, purpose limitation, transparency, and data minimization before the process goes live.

A privacy impact assessment, sometimes called a DPIA in some regulatory contexts, helps the organization evaluate the proposed data use, identify jurisdiction-specific obligations, and reduce unnecessary exposure. This approach is preventive because it checks the business process and data use before customers are affected.

Limited pilots, complaint monitoring, vendor accuracy claims, and simple removal of names are incomplete controls. They can be useful in other parts of an AI program, but they do not establish that the organization has a compliant basis to use customer data for a new AI-enabled personalization purpose. The AWS Privacy Reference Architecture provides current AWS guidance for designing privacy into workloads and managing privacy risks, including reducing unnecessary personal data exposure.

Question 6Skipped

A specialty grocery chain's executive committee has run AI pilots for inventory recommendations and customer offer personalization. Two pilots are now in production in a few regions and show measurable margin and retention improvements. National competitors are rapidly adding AI-enabled pricing, personalization, and store operations capabilities across their networks. The committee wants to set the next investment level without funding every AI idea equally.

Which investment approach should the committee choose?
Limit funding to isolated experiments until competitors disclose measurable AI returns in the market.
Explanation
This approach underinvests relative to the chain's current evidence and market pressure. Waiting for competitors to disclose results delays action even though the chain already has production use cases with measurable benefits.
Hold funding steady for pilots while requiring each team to select its preferred AI solution.
Explanation
This approach keeps the organization in experimentation mode even though some use cases have moved beyond pilots. Allowing each team to choose independently can fragment investment and weaken the ability to scale value consistently.
Correct answer
Increase funding to expand proven production use cases across priority markets and core operating teams.
Explanation
This approach matches the organization's maturity and the competitive environment. The chain has already shown measurable value in production, so the next investment level should expand proven use cases where they support business outcomes.
Shift funding to an enterprise-wide tool rollout before linking AI initiatives to business outcomes.
Explanation
This approach overinvests in broad deployment before confirming where value will come from. A technology-first rollout can increase cost and adoption risk because it does not prioritize use cases by measured business impact.
Overall explanation

AWS CAF-AI frames AI adoption as a maturity journey that should grow foundational capabilities while pursuing business value from AI, ML, and generative AI. When an organization has production AI use cases with measurable results and competitors are scaling similar capabilities, the investment level should move beyond isolated experimentation toward expanding proven areas of value.

The Launch stage focuses on moving early proofs of concept into production and demonstrating incremental business value. The Scale stage focuses on broadening production pilots into sustained value for customers and the business. In this scenario, the organization has already reached production evidence for selected use cases, so the best investment choice is to scale those validated use cases selectively rather than fund every idea or wait for more market proof.

AWS guidance also emphasizes working backward from expected business outcomes, stakeholders, measurable results, and required capabilities instead of starting from technology enthusiasm. Question 7Skipped

A national museum network has completed an introductory AI awareness course for curators, fundraising staff, visitor services, and operations managers. Surveys show that employees understand basic AI terminology but are unsure which day-to-day processes could benefit from AI. Leaders want the next workforce development step to turn awareness into applied judgment, include employees from several functions, and produce ideas that can be screened for value and risk.

Which approach should the leaders take?
Assign advanced ML courses with exams before staff identify process improvements.
Explanation
Advanced ML courses are deeper technical training than most business employees need to identify useful AI opportunities. The problem is translating basic awareness into relevant use cases, not requiring employees to master model-building content.
Correct answer
Run a facilitated AI hackathon around priority business challenges and prototype demos.
Explanation
A facilitated hackathon gives employees a structured way to apply AI concepts to real business challenges with peers from different functions. The activity can generate candidate use cases for later evaluation, without treating early prototypes as production-ready solutions.
Ask the central technology team to choose use cases and present finished pilots.
Explanation
A technology-led selection process can produce pilots, but it does not build broad AI literacy among business employees. It also reduces cross-functional engagement, which is important for discovering practical opportunities in daily work.
Open public GenAI tool access for teams to explore ideas without shared criteria.
Explanation
Unstructured public tool use can create unsafe habits and uneven learning. Without common business goals and review criteria, the activity is less likely to produce ideas that are suitable for value and risk screening.
Overall explanation

Workforce development should move employees from basic awareness toward applied AI literacy, where people can recognize suitable use cases, understand limitations, and collaborate responsibly with technical teams. A facilitated hackathon is useful when the goal is to create practical experience across functions while connecting ideas to business priorities and review criteria.

Hackathons work best as a structured learning and discovery mechanism, not as a shortcut to production deployment. Participants can explore concepts, build low-fidelity demonstrations, and learn from peers, while leaders collect ideas that can later be assessed for feasibility, value, risk, data readiness, and governance needs.

Mandatory advanced ML training can be valuable for technical roles, but it is usually too deep for enterprise-wide business literacy. Unrestricted experimentation is an anti-pattern because it encourages inconsistent practices and may bypass responsible AI expectations. A central team can help evaluate and mature ideas, but if that team does all the discovery, the organization misses the literacy and engagement benefits. Question 10Skipped

A nonprofit food bank's operations team wants to forecast weekly grocery box demand at each distribution site. The team has three years of historical scheduling data, site details, local events, weather, donation levels, actual requests, and final fulfillment counts recorded after weekly allocation decisions. Which approach should the team take to prepare the historical data for model training?
Correct answer
Use only pre-planning fields as inputs. Train with information available at forecast time.
Explanation
This is correct because training inputs should match the information that will be known when future forecasts are made. Fields that are created after weekly planning can make training results look stronger than the model will achieve in real planning use.
Combine all distribution sites first. Train without separate site-level attributes.
Explanation
This is incorrect because the team needs forecasts for each distribution site. Removing site-level attributes can hide important differences in demand patterns across neighborhoods and reduce the usefulness of the forecast for local planning.
Use only the latest weekly records. Train with data that reflects the current season.
Explanation
This is incorrect because using only the latest records can discard useful history about sites, events, weather, and recurring demand patterns. Current data is useful, but it should not automatically replace the broader historical record without a business reason.
Include final fulfillment counts as inputs. Train with the most complete historical record.
Explanation
This is incorrect because final fulfillment counts are recorded after allocation decisions and can reflect supply constraints, not only community demand. Using those counts as inputs can leak information that will not exist when the team needs to make a forecast.
Overall explanation

When historical data is used to train an AI model, each training example should be built from the information that would have been available at the time the business decision was made. This avoids data leakage, which happens when training inputs contain information from the future or from the outcome itself.

In this scenario, final fulfillment counts are created after allocation decisions and may reflect available supply, operational choices, or shortages. Those fields can make the model appear accurate during training, but they are not available when the food bank needs to forecast future demand. A stronger approach is to use pre-planning fields as inputs and keep the training process aligned with the real forecast moment. Which KPI approach should the team use?
Score the pilot mainly on latency, answer relevancy, hallucination rate, and API cost per proposal draft.
Explanation
These measures can help assess the assistant, but they do not show whether the initiative changes proposal costs, productivity, or sales outcomes. Technical measures that are disconnected from business impact can make a pilot look successful without proving that the use case creates value.
Deploy the assistant across all practice groups and calculate ROI from usage volume and monthly AI service spend.
Explanation
A broad launch before KPI validation treats the pilot as a deployment rather than a business validation exercise. Usage volume and service spend do not prove that proposals are cheaper, faster, better, or more likely to win.
Correct answer
Map proposal objectives to measurable targets and measures across business value, user adoption, and AI quality.
Explanation
This approach starts with the business problem and translates the desired outcomes into measurable goals. It includes tangible outcomes such as proposal cost or revenue impact and intangible outcomes such as attorney productivity or satisfaction, while still including AI quality measures that help explain performance.
Survey attorneys after rollout and use adoption sentiment as the main success measure for expansion funding.
Explanation
Attorney sentiment and adoption are useful intangible indicators, but using them alone leaves cost and revenue outcomes undefined. Setting the main success measure only after rollout also delays the decision criteria until after the team has already funded and deployed the initiative.
Overall explanation

Effective AI KPIs should connect the business objective to measurable outcomes and to the technical or user measures that explain whether the solution is working. AWS guidance for AI and ML initiatives emphasizes defining the business problem, identifying the business value, and establishing an observable and quantifiable performance metric before moving forward: Data Science.

For a generative AI pilot, the KPI set should not be limited to model or system quality. This combination helps leaders make an evidence-based funding decision instead of approving an initiative because it performs well technically or because users simply like it.

A tax preparation software vendor's product strategy team uses a high-performing generative AI model to draft explanations of tax form errors for customer support. Use reinforcement fine-tuning to reward responses that earn high satisfaction scores.

Reinforcement fine-tuning uses feedback signals to teach a model what counts as a good response. This can be useful when success criteria can be evaluated, but it does not specifically transfer knowledge into a smaller model to improve efficiency.

Retrieval Augmented Generation adds retrieved enterprise context to a prompt at runtime, which helps ground answers in proprietary or current information without changing model parameters. Supervised fine-tuning adjusts model parameters by learning from labeled examples, which is useful for repeatable tasks with approved outputs. For the distributor, the missing ingredient is not more reference material or a smaller model, it is a way to shape responses toward measurable service and safety outcomes.

Explanation

Explanation
